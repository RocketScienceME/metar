The problem

METARs and TAFs look like a secret code

Everyone is told to "just learn them", but most guides are either a wall of tables or a short list that stops at "wind, visibility, clouds".

So you memorise a few groups, and then it breaks:

  • A report has VRB, CAVOK, VV002 or Q1013 and you are not sure what it means.
  • A TAF has BECMG, TEMPO and PROB30 and you cannot tell which one replaces the weather and which one does not.
  • You read a US report one day and a European one the next, and the units are different.
  • You freeze in an exam, a simulator briefing or a flying lesson, when you just need to read the weather and move on.

The code is not hard. It has a fixed order and a small set of building blocks. It just needs to be explained once, clearly, with the exceptions included.

How to read a METAR: one report, decoded

This is how the guide works. Each group of the report has its own colour, and each one is explained in plain English.

METAR LIMC 241320Z 17014G26KT 140V210 7000 -RA SCT015 BKN040 14/12 Q1006 TEMPO 3000 RA BKN010 METAR LIMC 241320Z 17014G26KT 140V210 7000 -RA SCT015 BKN040 14/12 Q1006 TEMPO 3000 RA BKN010

The METAR LIMC 241320Z decoded group by group
GroupMeaning
LIMCMilan Malpensa, Italy
241320ZDay 24, 13:20 UTC
17014G26KTWind from 170° true at 14 kt, gusts 26 kt
140V210Direction varies between 140° and 210°
7000Visibility 7 km
-RALight rain
SCT015 BKN040Scattered at 1,500 ft, broken at 4,000 ft
14/12Temperature 14 °C, dew point 12 °C
Q1006QNH 1006 hPa
TEMPO 3000 RA BKN010At times 3 km in moderate rain with a 1,000 ft ceiling

What it means: a gusty southerly wind (gusts 12 kt above the mean). The ceiling is 4,000 ft (the scattered layer does not count). Visibility about 4.3 SM, so the category is MVFR. The TEMPO says it can drop to IFR for short spells.

A TAF method, not just a table

The timeline method turns FM, BECMG, TEMPO and PROB into a picture you can draw in 30 seconds.

FM replaces everything. BECMG replaces what it mentions. TEMPO and PROB are possible visits.
